## Which NYC Airlines Run Late the Most? Here's What the Data Actually Shows

If you've ever stood at a gate watching your departure time slide further away, you've probably wondered whether some airlines are just worse about this than others. I looked into a public dataset covering every flight that left New York's three major airports (JFK, LaGuardia, and Newark) in 2013, over 300,000 flights total, to see whether the airline actually matters.

## The short answer is yes, and the gap isn't small

Among the five busiest carriers out of NYC that year, average departure delays ranged from about 9 minutes for Delta to nearly 20 minutes for ExpressJet. That's more than double. JetBlue and ExpressJet weren't just slower on average either. Their delays were also a lot less predictable. Half of Delta and American's flights left within a few minutes of schedule in either direction, but ExpressJet and JetBlue had far more flights running well behind.

I also checked whether that early gap tends to close by the time a flight lands, and it doesn't. Flights that leave late almost always arrive late too. There's basically no making up time in the air. So a late departure is a pretty reliable sign of a late arrival.

To make sure the roughly 10 minute gap between the best and worst carriers wasn't just noise, I ran a test that estimates how much that gap could shift if you looked at a different sample of flights. Even accounting for that, the gap held up. Delta reliably beat ExpressJet by somewhere around 9 to 11 minutes per flight.

## What this doesn't tell you

This part is easy to skip past, but it shouldn't be. The data can't tell you why ExpressJet ran later. Maybe ExpressJet just handles ground operations worse. Or maybe ExpressJet happens to fly more routes during the busiest hours of the day, and the traffic is doing the damage, not the airline. Sorting those two apart would take a different kind of analysis than this one.

It's also worth knowing that flights which got cancelled outright aren't in this data at all. There's no delay to measure for a flight that never left. That means these numbers are probably a best case picture of how bad 2013 delays really were, not a worst case one.

Bottom line, if you're booking out of NYC and want better odds of leaving on time, the carrier does matter. Delta and American were the safer bets in 2013.
